Here’s a concise summary of the key takeaways from this fact/dimension discussion, especially in the context of analytics pipelines:

---

## 1️⃣ Fact vs Dimension

* **Facts**: Event-based, high-volume, typically aggregated (`SUM`, `COUNT`, `AVG`).
* **Dimensions**: Snapshot/state info, used in `GROUP BY`, can be high or low cardinality.
* Blurry cases exist:

  * Example: Airbnb price → dim (settings) vs fact (price change event).
  * Boolean/existence flags (`dim_is_active`, `dim_bought_item`) → can be treated as dim or fact depending on aggregation and granularity.

---

## 2️⃣ Derived Dimensions

* Often **bucketized** from facts using `CASE WHEN` to reduce cardinality (<10 values).
* Examples:

  * Boolean/existence: `dim_is_active`, `dim_ever_booked`, `dim_has_ever_done_x`.
  * Temporal: `days_since_last_active`, `days_since_signup`.
* Once derived, changing them later is **expensive**, so plan carefully.

---

## 3️⃣ Categorical Dimensions

* Derived from one or multiple facts/conditions.
* Hard to recompute if stored; better to compute at ingestion if possible.
* Example: `dim_user_tier` based on cumulative spend or activity.

---

## 4️⃣ Date List / Compression

* Instead of storing repeated dates as arrays, use **compressed integer representation** (`datelist_int`) for space efficiency and faster querying.
* Example:

| user_id | date       | datelist_int    |
| ------- | ---------- | --------------- |
| 32      | 2023-01-01 | 100000010000001 |

* Keeps historical activity while optimizing storage.

---

## 5️⃣ Design Principles

* Facts: atomic, immutable, high-volume → idempotent ingestion pipelines.
* Dimensions: slowly changing or snapshot → SCD1/2/3 patterns.
* Derived flags or bucketized metrics: precompute at ingestion to reduce repeated computation downstream.
* Compression and storage efficiency are crucial for **high cardinality temporal dimensions**.
